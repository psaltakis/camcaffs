Most kitchens here publish no dietary labels, so where a dish carries none we read it from its name. Those colours are our guess, not the kitchen's word, and a recipe can always change. If it matters to you, ask at the counter.

Menus are copied automatically from each kitchen's own public pages every 30 minutes. They change on the day, run out, and are sometimes published late or not at all. Serving times and guest rules come from each college's own pages and are often different in vacations.

What this page stores, and what it does not

No cookies. No analytics, no trackers, no accounts, no advertising. Nothing you do here is sent to us, and there is no us to send it to: the page is one file and a script that reads public menu pages.

It does keep a few things in your browser's own local storage, so the page behaves the way you left it: the dietary filters you picked, light or dark mode, whether you have asked for walking distances, and a copy of the last menus it loaded so the screen is not blank while the next lot arrive. That stays on your device. You can wipe it whenever you like.

Location. Nothing happens until you press Near me. Your browser then asks you, and if you agree your position is used inside your browser to sort the places by distance. It is never sent to us or to anyone else, and it is never written down. Directions links open Google Maps with the destination only.

The menus themselves are fetched from a Google Apps Script address, so Google sees that request in the same way your internet provider sees every other page you open.

The map is the one part that reaches further. If you open it, the page loads the Leaflet library from cdnjs and the map itself from OpenStreetMap. Those two see your IP address and which part of the map you looked at, as any website you visit would. Nothing is loaded from them unless you open the map, and nothing about you is sent with the request.
